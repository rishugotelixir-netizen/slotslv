About DIY & Crafts

We publish project guides that hold up in a real workshop — written by people who actually built the thing, measured twice, and photographed the mistakes.

Our Mission

To make hands-on making accessible. Most DIY advice online skips the awkward parts: the wrong saw, the split board, the finish that stays tacky for a week. We document those failures alongside the successes, so a first-time builder spends less money on mistakes and more time making something worth keeping.

Our Vision

A library of well-documented projects that stay useful years later — guides with clear materials lists, real time estimates, and repair notes. We want people to build a habit of making rather than a habit of buying, and to feel confident picking up a tool they have never held before.

Principles We Follow

  • Honest difficulty: If a project needs a table saw, we say so. We do not label a project beginner when it is not.
  • Materials first: Every guide opens with a list and a cost estimate, including what can be substituted.
  • Safety always: Required protective gear and ventilation are noted before the first cut, not buried at the end.
  • Fixable mistakes: We explain how to recover from common errors instead of pretending they do not happen.

How We Test

Each project is built at least once in a home workshop before it goes live. We record the real elapsed time, the tools actually used, and the cuts that did not go to plan. Nothing is published from a tutorial draft alone. Corrections from readers go back into the guide so the next person benefits.

Who We Are

A small remote team of woodworkers, sewists, and repair hobbyists who got tired of searching for the same information. We work across different crafts and budgets on purpose — a repair guide and a beginner woodworking project should feel like the same source of careful, repeatable instructions.

What We Are Working On

The library is growing in every direction: more repair guides that keep appliances and furniture in use, printable project plans with dimensioned drawings, and short technique videos for the steps that are hard to describe in text. We are also opening community submissions so readers can share a build that turned out well. Everything stays free to read, and every guide keeps a revision history so you can see when it was last checked.
