Your First Five Woodworking Projects

Build in an order that teaches one skill at a time instead of five at once.

Published 2025-08-22

The reason most people abandon woodworking is not a lack of skill. It is starting with a project that needs six techniques they have never used. These five projects, in this order, each add one thing.

1. A Simple Cutting Board

Teaches measuring, marking, and a clean cut. Buy a wide board, rip it to size, and sand through the grits. The finish is optional, which makes it a good place to stop if your interest fades.

2. A Small Shelf or Box

Adds joinery. Glue and screws are enough for a beginner, and a square box teaches you to check edges instead of assuming they are right.

3. A Simple Stool or Step

Introduces structural thinking. Load matters here, so follow a tested design rather than improvising — the dimensions are load-bearing, not aesthetic.

4. A Frame of Any Kind

Picture frame, mirror frame, or small wall mount. Miter joints are the real lesson, and a practice cut on offcut timber is worth the ten minutes.

5. Something You Will Actually Use

Now pick the object you have wanted for months. By this point you have the core skills; the project is harder but no longer unfamiliar territory.
