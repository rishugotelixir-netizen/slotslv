Upcycling Things You Would Normally Bin

Most of what gets thrown out still has one good part in it.

Published 2025-08-22

The cheapest material is usually already in the house or free outside it. Reuse works best when you judge the item before you commit to the project, not after you have cut it open.

Pallets and Crates

Look for clean, unstained boards with no splits or protruding nails. A pallet is a stack of offcuts — take it apart rather than trying to build from it whole, and always pull nails with the grain so the wood does not split.

Drawers and Cabinet Parts

Old drawers hide solid timber bottoms, dovetail corners, and hardware that costs more new. If the joinery is intact, the part is usually better than modern sheet material of the same size.

Glass Jars and Containers

Wide-mouth jars make good storage, lamps, and planters. Remove labels with warm soapy water and a scraper rather than solvents, and check for chips around the rim before you commit.

Strip Safely

Old paint may contain lead, especially on furniture made before the 1980s. Test before scraping, wear a respirator, work outside, and never sand old coatings without protection. If you are unsure, take the item to a hazardous waste facility.

Know When to Stop

Some items are not worth the effort: warped timber, soft or crumbly wood, rusted-through metal, and anything with hidden pest damage. Knowing the point of no return saves more time than any project tip.
