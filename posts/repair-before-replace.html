Repair Before You Replace

An hour of fixing usually beats a new purchase — with a clear line for when it does not.

Published 2025-08-22

Most things written off as broken have one loose screw, one clogged filter, or one worn part. Diagnosis takes ten minutes; the repair usually takes less time than the trip to buy a replacement.

Furniture: Joints First

Wobbly chairs and tables fail at the joint, not the leg. Disassemble the loose joint, clean out the old glue, add fresh wood glue, and clamp it square. Squeaking often means the same fix, plus a little wax on the contact surface.

Hinges and Handles

Stripped screw holes in wood or particleboard are filled with a hardwood dowel, re-drilled, and fitted with a slightly longer screw. This works on door handles, drawer runners, and cabinet hinges alike.

Seams and Upholstery

A torn seam is a stitch before it is a reupholstery job. Match thread weight and colour closely, backstitch at each end, and pull the original seam into alignment rather than starting a new line.

Appliances and Small Machines

Unplug first, every time. Blocked vents, lint build-up, and a tripped breaker from accumulated dust are common and fixable. Only open the casing if you are comfortable with mains electricity — otherwise stop and call a technician.

When Not to Repair

Stop when the failure is structural in a safety-critical part, when a seal or bearing is gone, or when the repair cost approaches a new unit's price. Knowing the limit is a skill, not a failure.
