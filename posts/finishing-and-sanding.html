Finishing Wood Without Regret

Preparation decides the result. The topcoat is the easy part.

Published 2025-08-22

Stains that blotch, finishes that stay tacky, and edges that feel like sandpaper all share one cause: preparation. Get this sequence right and almost any finish behaves.

Sand Through the Grits

Work progressively — 120, then 180, then 220. Skipping a grit leaves scratches that show up under finish and cannot be fixed without starting over. Sand with the grain, not across it.

Break the Edge

Every sharp arris will chip during handling and shows through any finish. A light knock with sandpaper or a piece of scrap timber rounds it without changing the profile.

Clean Before Coating

Dust off with a tack cloth or damp rag and let the surface dry fully. Applying finish over fine dust is what produces that rough, sandpaper feel in the first place.

Choose the Finish by Use

Oil for a warm, repairable surface that can be refreshed. Wax for low-traffic pieces. Film-forming lacquer or paint for surfaces that get wet or wiped often — and accept that touch-ups must be done with the same product.

Thin the First Coat

On bare wood, the first coat soaks in and can blotch or go patchy. A slightly thinner first application followed by two normal coats gives an even, durable result.
