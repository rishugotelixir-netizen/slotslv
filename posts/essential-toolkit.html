The Minimal Tool Kit

Six tools cover most home projects. Here is the honest order to buy them.

Published 2025-08-22

Tool advice usually starts with a wishlist. We are starting with the opposite: the smallest set that lets you finish real projects, and what to leave on the shelf for now.

Buy First

A tape measure, a speed square, a combination square, a block plane, a hand saw, and a set of screwdrivers. Total cost is a fraction of a single power tool and this set will outlast the machines you add later.

Borrow Next

Drill, jigsaw, sander, and a circular saw are worth borrowing until you know which you will use weekly. Many hardware stores and tool libraries rent by the day, which is enough to finish a project list.

Buy When You Need It

A miter saw earns its space quickly if you build frames regularly. A router, planer, or table saw is a later purchase — with a sharp plane, a good saw, and patience you can cover most cuts without them.

Do Not Skip

Safety glasses, a clamp or two, and sharp blades. A dull blade is the most common cause of splintering and tear-out, and it is also the most common reason beginners get injured.
